# General red flag indicators

## Customer behaviour
Reluctance to provide identifying information or explain the purpose of a
transaction; providing information that cannot be verified or that conflicts
with prior statements; unusual concern with reporting thresholds or record
retention; conducting transactions through intermediaries without apparent
reason; and abruptly changing behaviour after being asked routine questions.

## Account activity
Activity with no apparent business or lawful purpose; transactions inconsistent
with the customer's stated occupation, income, or business type; sudden and
unexplained changes in volume or value; funds moving through an account with
minimal balance retained; and multiple accounts with no evident need.

## Transaction characteristics
Frequent round-number amounts; amounts just below reporting thresholds; rapid
movement of funds in and out; transfers between accounts with no apparent
relationship; and payment descriptions that are absent, generic, or
inconsistent with the amount involved.

## Corporate and entity indicators
Complex ownership structures with no commercial rationale; entities registered
in one jurisdiction that operate entirely in another; nominee directors or
shareholders; entities with no discernible operations; and shared registered
addresses across many unrelated entities.

## Weighting
No single indicator establishes suspicion. Indicators are weighed together and
against the customer's documented profile. A pattern that is unremarkable for
one customer type may be highly unusual for another, and the analysis must be
explicit about that comparison.
