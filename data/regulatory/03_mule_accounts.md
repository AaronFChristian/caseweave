# Typology: money mule accounts and funnel activity

## Definition
A money mule receives funds from multiple unrelated parties into a personal
account and forwards them onward, typically in consolidated form and often
cross-border. Mules may be witting participants, recruited accomplices, or
victims of employment or romance fraud who do not understand their role.

## Observable indicators
- Numerous small credits from a large number of unrelated senders over a short
  period, followed by one or a few large debits.
- Credits inconsistent with the account holder's income or history.
- The account holder is young, newly onboarded, or recently changed contact
  details.
- Onward transfers to a single beneficiary, frequently in a higher-risk
  jurisdiction.
- Balance returning to near zero after each consolidation cycle.

## Common benign explanations
Rotating savings and credit associations, group gift collections, community
fundraising, shared household expenses, and small informal resale businesses
all produce many-to-one credit patterns. The volume, the absence of any
relationship between senders, and the speed and destination of the onward
transfer are what distinguish mule activity.

## Victim consideration
Where indicators suggest the account holder may be a fraud victim rather than a
participant, that assessment should be recorded. It affects both the narrative
framing and the institution's customer treatment.
