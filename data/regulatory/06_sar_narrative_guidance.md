# Drafting the suspicious activity report narrative

## Purpose
The narrative is the most important part of a suspicious activity report. It is
the section a law enforcement analyst reads first and often the only section
they read. It must allow a reader with no access to the filing institution's
systems to understand what happened and why the institution found it suspicious.

## Required structure
A complete narrative addresses six questions in a logical order:

- **Who** — the subjects, including full identifying details, account numbers,
  relationships between subjects, and their role in the activity.
- **What** — the instruments and mechanisms involved, with amounts.
- **When** — the period of the activity, with specific dates.
- **Where** — the branches, jurisdictions, and channels used.
- **Why** — the specific reasons the institution considers the activity
  suspicious, expressed against the customer's known profile.
- **How** — the sequence in which the activity occurred.

## Style requirements
Write in plain chronological English. Introduce each subject fully on first
mention and use a consistent short reference afterwards. State amounts and
dates precisely. Do not use internal jargon, unexplained abbreviations, or
system codes. Do not include the institution's internal alert identifiers as
though they were evidence.

## What must not appear
The narrative must not state a legal conclusion. Write that activity is
consistent with a described typology, not that a subject committed an offence.
Do not speculate beyond the evidence, do not include information the
institution has not verified, and do not include material unrelated to the
suspicion. Every factual assertion should be traceable to a specific
transaction, account record, or document in the institution's possession.

## Confidentiality
The existence of a suspicious activity report, and any information that would
reveal its existence, is confidential and must not be disclosed to the subject
or to any unauthorised person. This obligation is statutory and applies to the
filing institution and its personnel.
