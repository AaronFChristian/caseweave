# Typology: structuring and CTR avoidance

## Definition
Structuring is the deliberate division of a currency transaction into multiple
smaller transactions to evade a reporting threshold. In the United States a
financial institution must file a Currency Transaction Report for cash
transactions exceeding $10,000 in a single business day. Splitting $27,000 into
four deposits of roughly $6,750 across three days is structuring, and the act of
structuring is itself a federal offence independent of the source of the funds.

## Observable indicators
- Multiple cash deposits or withdrawals just below the reporting threshold.
- Deposits made at several branches or ATMs on the same day.
- Deposits split across multiple accounts controlled by the same person.
- Cash activity inconsistent with the customer's stated occupation or the
  expected activity recorded at onboarding.
- A customer who asks about the reporting threshold and then reduces the size
  of a pending transaction.

## Common benign explanations
Cash-intensive legitimate businesses (restaurants, car washes, laundromats,
used-vehicle dealers, convenience stores) bank real cash daily and frequently
present amounts in the $7,000 to $10,000 range. Insurance and payroll cycles
can also cluster deposits. An analyst must reconcile the pattern against the
customer's declared business type and expected activity profile before
concluding intent.

## Evidence an investigator should assemble
Deposit-by-deposit listing with amounts, dates, times and locations; the
customer's declared occupation or business type; expected activity from the
customer due diligence file; any prior alerts or filings on the same subject;
and whether a single individual conducted the deposits or several did.
